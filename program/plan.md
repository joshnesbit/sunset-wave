# A Wave of 100 Gatherings
**Saturday, March 20, 2027 — the spring equinox, all day.** 100 small gatherings across the Outer Sunset. $200 per host. No permits, nothing formal.

Sponsored by the **Sunset Social Club**. Open to any Outer Sunset resident — membership not required.

Lineage: this is the **Microgrant Gathering** recipe (Connective Tissue / Trust for Civic Life), run at 100 × $200. Their Charlottesville pilot budgeted for 30 hosts, expected five applications, got 70. Two lessons carried over: demand outruns supply when saying yes takes three minutes instead of an application, and reporting is one photo and one sentence.

## Connection type
**Bridging first, bonding as the byproduct.** The point is the neighbor you wave at but have never met. So: low-stakes first move (walk to the end of your block), a host whose job is to make strangers feel expected (the sidewalk sign does most of that), and the day itself as the warm introduction. Blocks that already gather get a bonding rhythm out of it too — but the design optimizes for the stranger.

## The two artifacts, and what they're for
- **The host invitation** (`materials/host-invitation.html`) — the printed page you hand to a club member, a parent at the school gate, a neighbor on the sidewalk. It has one job: turn a reader into a host. Letter size, prints as-is.
- **The Wave map** (the app) — where "I'll host" lands and the wave becomes visible. The count is the persuasion; the coral tile is the reward.

## People & practices
The map is 10% of this. The 90% is below.

**Avenue captains.** Six to eight people, each holding a stretch of avenues (19th–24th, 25th–30th, and so on), each personally shepherding 12–15 hosts: a text when they sign up, a knock the week before, a visit on the day. The map's job is to tell captains who to call. Recruit captains first, before the map goes public.

**The first ten hosts are invited personally**, before anyone sees the map — so it never shows zero. 48th & Irving is the first coral tile.

**Host Night — Saturday, March 6 at 4114 Judah**, two weeks out. Hosts pick up their $200 and a printed sidewalk sign, and meet the other hosts on their avenue. The grant handoff is in person on purpose: it makes the money a gathering instead of a transaction, and it introduces hosts who live four blocks apart.

**Seeding order.** Private channels first (mutual aid pod, block neighbors, moms group, dads group, guys group), in Josh's voice, with the invitation attached. Then the club. Then Outer Sunset Today and the Community Guide. The public map never surfaces the private channels.

**Translation as relationship.** A Chinese-language invitation translated by a bilingual neighbor and credited by name on the page — not machine-translated. Spanish the same way if a neighbor offers.

**Rain.** Rain or shine; an open garage is the classic Sunset gathering anyway. A one-week slide to March 27 only for a real storm, called by the Thursday before.

**Afterward.** One photo, one line, from each host. That's the report, and it becomes the record.

## Where this sits in the ecosystem
New, and temporary by design — a campaign, not a standing tool. It neighbors **Outer Sunset Today** (which should list the day's gatherings once there are enough), the **Community Guide**, and **Cozy Corner** (whose block is the prototype for what a good tile looks like). It duplicates nothing: no existing tool holds "who is hosting on which block on one specific day."
