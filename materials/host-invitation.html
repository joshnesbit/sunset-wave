What hosting actually is

  • Pick your spot. Your driveway, your front steps, the sidewalk, a corner of the park. Wherever people can see you from the street.
  • Pick your hour. Morning coffee, an afternoon grill, chalk and lemonade after nap time. All day means you choose.
  • Put something out. Donuts and a coffee urn is a real gathering. So is a pot of soup, or a table of art supplies.
  • Make a sign. "Come by — neighbors welcome." We'll print you one. That sign is most of the work.
  • Tell four neighbors. Knock, or drop a note. The ones you wave at but have never met — those are the ones.
  • Afterward, send one photo and one sentence. That's the whole report.

Why this Saturday, and why now

A few months after a long election season, this is a way of saying we're still a neighborhood without having to say it. Nobody gives a speech. You just end up on the sidewalk with people who live near you, and afterward you know a name you didn't know before.
